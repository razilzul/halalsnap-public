Why so many labels come back doubtful

More than a third of the products HalalSnap read in the last seven weeks came back doubtful. Most were not doubtful because of anything haram on the label. They were doubtful because one line of the label never says where an ingredient came from.

36%
of 768 product reads came back doubtful (21 Aug 2026 to 11 Oct 2026)
79%
of those named an ingredient whose plant or animal source was the open question
58%
had exactly one flagged line, and it was a source question

The ingredients behind the doubt

When HalalSnap says doubtful, it names the ingredient that decided it. Grouping those names across 277 doubtful reads gives a short list. A read can name more than one group.

The top four are all the same question. E471 and its relatives, glycerin and lecithin can each be made from plant oils or from animal fat, and the finished ingredient looks the same either way. Gelatin is always animal: the question is which animal and how it was slaughtered. Outside food, in cosmetics, toothpaste and personal care, glycerin alone accounts for 35 of the 44 doubtful reads.

Why HalalSnap does not just assume the plant version

Most lecithin is made from soy. Much E471 is made from palm oil. It would be easy to say "usually plant, so probably fine". HalalSnap used to reason that way, and on 11 August 2026 a real uncertified sandwich came back more permissive than the evidence allowed. What is common across an industry is not evidence about the maker of the pack in your hand, so an unstated source now stays doubtful until the pack settles it.

That change shows in the numbers. Before 21 Aug 2026, 11% of 645 reads came back doubtful. Since then it is 36%. The labels did not get worse. The rules stopped filling the gap with a guess.

It is not only HalalSnap

Singapore's MUIS publishes a Food Additive Listing for certifiers. It marks 104 of the 288 additives it lists as syubhah, meaning a certifier asks the maker to document the source or the manufacturing process before certifying a product. That list is a verification step for certifiers in Singapore, dated 2016, not a ruling that every product containing those additives is doubtful. But it makes the same point: an additive's code alone often cannot tell you its source.

HalalSnap does not copy that list into a verdict. It lowers an answer only where the realistic source could be animal, such as emulsifiers, glycerin, lecithin and some flavour enhancers. Additives MUIS flags for their manufacturing process, such as citric acid and MSG, are not raised on their own. Each one has its own page in the ingredient reference.

What settles it

  • A recognised halal mark on the pack in your hand settles the whole product.
  • A vegan or vegetarian mark settles animal-source questions.
  • A source written right next to the ingredient settles that ingredient: "soy lecithin", "vegetable glycerin", "E471 (vegetable)".
  • "Palm oil" elsewhere in the list does not. It belongs to the oil, not to the emulsifier.

In 58% of the doubtful reads, one of those could have been enough. That is why HalalSnap asks you to check one line rather than telling you to put the product back.

How these numbers were made

The figures come from HalalSnap's product memory, read on 11 Oct 2026. A scan is saved there only when the full ingredient list was read, the photo quality was high and the answer was confident. The table holds no user account or device data; besides the product, it keeps only the market (for example SG), the school of thought and the language. 1413 reads were saved between 25 Jun 2026 and 11 Oct 2026; the main figures use the 768 saved from 21 Aug 2026, when the current additive rules took effect. Ingredient groups were counted by matching the flagged ingredient names, which the app writes in the reader's language, against patterns for each group, so the counts are close rather than exact. No brand or product is named, and no figure here describes a single person.

Questions

Does doubtful mean haram?

No. Doubtful means the label leaves a real question open, usually where one ingredient came from. It is a reason to check, not a ruling that the product is prohibited.

Why would the same product be fine with a vegan mark?

A vegan or vegetarian mark rules out an animal source, which is the open question for most emulsifiers, glycerin and lecithin. HalalSnap accepts that mark as settling it.

Where do these numbers come from?

Product reads HalalSnap saved between 21 Aug 2026 and 11 Oct 2026. Only reads with a complete ingredient list, a clear photo and high confidence are saved, and they hold no user identity.
