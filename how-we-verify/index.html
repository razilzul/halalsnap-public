Trust · How we test every update

We caught our AI claiming a halal certification it could not see.

It was answering from brand memory, not the photo. We fixed it, re-tested until it stopped, and only then shipped. That is the bar every HalalSnap update has to clear before it reaches you. This page shows you the bar.

The question is yours. The homework is ours.

"Is this halal?" is your question, and you can point it at anything you eat, drink, or put on your body: food, drinks, supplements, cosmetics, toothpaste. HalalSnap's job is to read what is actually in front of the camera and report what settled scholarship and your authority's published standards say about it, with the reasoning shown.

An app that answers a question like that has one duty above all others: it must never claim more than the evidence in front of it. So before any update ships, we spend our time trying to make it fail.

The standard behind all of it
In every test round, it never called a product more permissible than the evidence in front of it. It is allowed to be cautious. It is never allowed to be generous with your deen.

The catch we are glad we made

During testing, the new engine did something that looked small and was not. It claimed a product carried halal certification. There was no certification anywhere in the photo. It recognised the brand and answered from memory, the way a person might say "oh, that brand is certified" without looking at the pack in their hand.

Brands change suppliers. Certifications lapse. The pack in your hand is the only pack that counts.

So we wrote a permanent rule into the engine: memory is never allowed to establish certification. Only what is visible in the photo can. Then we re-tested until the false claim happened zero times in ten attempts, shipped, and checked again on the live service. Still zero.

Testing can never promise forever. What it can do is prove the bar, and that rule now guards every scan you take.

When it is not sure, you will know

Point it at a product with no readable label and it will not pretend. It tells you what it is assuming and how confident it can be. When an ingredient needs source verification, it says so and points you to the manufacturer or a trusted authority. Certification only counts when it can actually be seen.

And yes, it got faster too: the AI analysis itself now takes about 7 seconds on a typical product, measured July 2026, roughly 40% quicker than before. Speed was the easy part. The testing above is why we shipped it.

No scanner can promise certainty. HalalSnap grades the evidence it can see, shows its reasoning, and tells you when an ingredient needs a closer look. For a final ruling, ask your scholar. For certification, check the certifying body. The decision stays yours.
