Is vanilla extract halal?

Vanilla extract

The short answer
Likely halal

Likely halal. The alcohol is a carrier that dissolves to negligibility in the finished food (istihlak).

What vanilla extract is

Vanilla extract is made by soaking vanilla beans in alcohol. In a cake or ice cream it is a trace carrier. Drinking the bottle would be a different question.

By mazhab

Why it depends

The alcohol is a carrier that dissolves to negligibility in the finished food (istihlak). It is not from khamr and cannot intoxicate in any realistic amount, so major contemporary authorities tolerate it. The schools differ on how cautious to be, and the markets that publish a figure use their own figure.

How to check the pack

  • A halal mark settles it. "Vanilla flavouring" or vanillin made without alcohol avoids the question.
  • Look for a recognised halal mark (JAKIM, MUI/BPJPH, MUIS or another reputable certifier) on the pack in your hand. A mark you can see settles it for the whole product.R-CERT-01
  • Still unsure? Ask the maker which source they use, or choose a certified alternative.

Questions people ask

What is vanilla extract?

Vanilla extract is made by soaking vanilla beans in alcohol. In a cake or ice cream it is a trace carrier. Drinking the bottle would be a different question.

Is vanilla extract halal or haram?

Likely halal. The alcohol is a carrier that dissolves to negligibility in the finished food (istihlak).

Is vanilla extract halal in Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia?

Singapore: The MUIS fatwa on ethanol allows it only as a flavouring solvent: up to 0.5% in the flavouring and 0.1% in the final product, from a non-khamr source. MUIS also lists E1510 as syubhah in its 2016 additive list. Malaysia: Malaysia's 2011 National Fatwa Council muzakarah, applied by JAKIM: alcohol added to carry a flavour or colour must not come from liquor making and must not exceed 0.5% in the final product. Naturally occurring alcohol has no fixed limit. Drinks not made as liquor are accepted below 1%. Indonesia: MUI Fatwa 10 of 2018: non-khamr ethanol in food has no numeric limit if it is not harmful. A drink at or above 0.5% ethanol is khamr. Ethanol from the khamr industry is haram at any level.
