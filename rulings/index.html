Open method

How HalalSnap decides a verdict

Every rule the app follows, written out in full. Point a camera at a product and this is the logic that produces the answer — including where scholars differ, and how we handle it when they do.

  • 98rules in force
  • 69of them fiqh rulings
  • 54test scans per release
  • 6possible verdicts

Two limits, stated up front. These rules describe what the app concludes when it has read a label correctly — not how well it reads. Misreading a blurry photo is a separate kind of error, and the test scans at the end are what guard against it.

And the app gives a reading, not a fatwa. Where scholars genuinely differ, these rules follow the user's own school and their own national halal authority rather than pick a winner.

What we checked, and what we found

Every rule checked line by line against the app's live instructions; every sourced number and named authority re-verified against its primary source or the project's own provenance document.

Checked against production. Verified on 25 July 2026 against the then-current function, and superseded. Production changed on 11 and 12 August 2026 (issues #552, #553, #555). Re-verified on 20 August 2026: production analyze-food v163 was downloaded and compared against main and is identical, and this table has been corrected to match it. The rules marked PENDING REVIEW below describe live behaviour that has not yet been ruled on, not behaviour the app fails to follow.

This table covers the rules that decide a VERDICT. The app's instructions also contain writing and tone rules — how warm to be, when humour is and is not allowed, length limits — which shape how an answer reads but never what it concludes. Those are deliberately not listed here.

Where a claim did not fully hold up, it has been left in place and marked with an audit note rather than quietly corrected — you can see what we found and judge it yourself. Open items:

  • R-ADD-02 — carmine in cosmetics is genuinely undecided and still needs a ruling.
  • R-SEA-03 — the Hanafi position on crab is usually stated as makruh tahrimi, a distinct category; the app currently uses 'Doubtful'. Your view is wanted.
  • Which authority should a Muslim living away from home follow — the one where they live, or the one they grew up under? A Malaysian in London: JAKIM or HMC/HFA? The app currently infers the market from the phone's language setting and applies that market's authority, without telling the user or letting them change it. Your answer decides whether that should stay automatic or become the user's choice.
  • When a label does not state whether an ingredient came from a plant or an animal, and the user's own authority (JAKIM, MUIS, MUI and so on) has no published ruling on that ingredient, what should the app do? The proposal is: follow the authority wherever it has actually ruled, and otherwise treat the source as doubtful only where a genuinely haram source is realistic in today's supply for that ingredient and that market. On that test E471 is doubtful because tallow is realistic, while vitamin D3 stays likely halal because its realistic worst case is lanolin. The app currently decides this per ingredient with no single stated test, which is how R-ADD-04 and R-ADD-09 ended up disagreeing.
  • Following on from the above: R-ADD-05, R-ADD-09, R-FORT-02 and R-FORT-04 are all the same situation as R-ADD-04 and all still answer it more leniently. They are marked PENDING REVIEW and should be settled together, in one pass, rather than one at a time.
  • R-SURF-02: should reading more of a label be allowed to make a verdict stricter? Front-of-pack, a chocolate bar is likely halal. Turn it over so the emulsifier is readable and it becomes doubtful. Both answers are defensible; they should not both be accidents.
  • E901 beeswax and E904 shellac: MUIS marks both Syubhah. Their source is known (insect), so this is the insect question rather than the unstated-source question, and it needs a ruling rather than a rule.
  • The strict source rule is currently drawn from MUIS alone. JAKIM and MUI publish their own lists and have not been captured. Until they are, HalalSnap applies one authority's evidence to every market as its own ruling. Is that the right call, or should the rule be market-scoped?
  • The app holds one authority additive listing (MUIS). JAKIM, MUI and the UK bodies are not captured, so outside Singapore the app states the source question and names nobody. Should HalalSnap seek those published lists, and what should it say in a market where no list exists?

The six verdicts

Every scan ends on exactly one of these. The rule throughout is to use the least severe verdict the evidence honestly supports.

VerdictWhat it meansWhat the app advises
HalalClear permissibility with no meaningful concern, or a recognised halal certification mark is visible on the pack in the photo.Use
Likely halalNo clear prohibited ingredient. At most a minor or well-tolerated uncertainty remains — typically an ingredient whose exact source the label does not state.Use
DoubtfulA real, practical, unresolved concern — an unknown animal source, unclear slaughter, or a serious concern raised by the user's school or national authority.Verify / caution
HaramA clearly prohibited ingredient is present, or the use itself is clearly impermissible.Avoid
Not recommendedPermissible in substance but discouraged. Used mainly for entertainment and media that clashes with Islamic values without being clearly prohibited.Caution
UnrecognisableThe photo is too poor to identify anything at all. Not a ruling — a request for a better photo.Retake the photo

Principles above every individual rule

PrincipleStatement
Least severe accurate verdictThe app must always use the least severe verdict the evidence honestly supports. Inflating severity 'to be safe' is treated as an error, not caution.
Evidence hierarchyApplied top-down: (1) a halal certification mark visible in this photo; (2) the position of the user's own national halal authority; (3) the methodology of the user's selected mazhab; (4) general caution principles, last.
Waswas guardA concern must be real, present and practical before it may lower a verdict. The app is instructed not to invent concerns.
Inference is never verificationThe app may not claim a product, ingredient source, factory or certification has been verified unless it is visible in the photo, stated by the user, or supplied by the backend. Everything else must be phrased as likelihood.
ConsistencyThe same label must never receive two different verdicts on two different scans. Rules marked MECHANICAL below are pinned for this reason.

2 rules

R-CONF-01Every scan also reports a confidence figure next to the verdict.Always(a measure of certainty, NOT a measure of how halal something is)Confidence measures how sure the app is of the verdict it just gave. The dietary 'Watch for' feature is NOT a religious ruling

Separately from the halal verdict, users may switch on personal watches for allergens, sugar, keto and pregnancy. These answer health questions off the same photo and never change the halal verdict. They are listed in the test-case sheet marked 'Health watch' so it is clear the app is not issuing religious rulings on them. Pregnancy guidance follows public health sources (NHS / MOH-style guidance on listeria, raw egg, caffeine and high-dose liquorice), not fiqh.
